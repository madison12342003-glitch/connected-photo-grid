# Photo Layer Rules

## Selection
Review every available photograph before choosing the nine primary images. Do not select by upload order. Consider clarity, documentary value, subject diversity, lighting, color, crop flexibility, and contribution to the overall story.

## Preserve reality
- Treat user photographs as the factual source of place and culture.
- Preserve recognizable subjects, natural light, weather, and material texture.
- Do not fabricate mountains, buildings, animals, signs, religious objects, or local details inside a documentary photograph.
- Avoid aggressive smoothing, synthetic HDR, excessive sharpening, or global recoloring that makes the scene look artificial.
- If a photograph contains legible signs or culturally meaningful details, do not distort or replace them.

## Cropping
For each candidate, record the focal subject, safe crop area, horizon height, movement direction, and edge anchors. Avoid cropping faces, buildings, prayer flags, flowers, or mountain peaks unintentionally. When an edge is intended to connect to another tile, plan the crop using the actual edge geometry.

## Secondary photo use
A second photograph can appear as a small inset, overlapping print, narrow strip, or detail window. Use a consistent treatment for multiple insets. Avoid adding a second photo merely to fill empty space.

## Selection record
For each image, record:
- source ID or filename
- subject and scene
- dominant colors
- focal point and crop constraints
- edge features on all four sides
- documentary importance
- likely tile role
- whether it is selected, backup, or rejected and why
