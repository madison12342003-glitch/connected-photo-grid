# Overlapping Photomontage Direction

## Art direction target
Create a cool, restrained, slightly classical/editorial Sichuan–Tibetan travel collage from the user's actual photos—not a tourism brochure, not tidy photo cards, and not an all-grayscale montage.

- Palette: clean neutral/cool near-white, black/ink, restrained red.
- Match the background to pale sky, snow, mist, and grey-white photo edges. Avoid yellowed paper, beige, vintage paper grain, and oversized empty gutters.
- Preserve the source set's muted natural colors. Apply modest selective saturation/contrast changes rather than vivid poster color or heavy desaturation.
- Mood: confident, cinematic, layered, elegant, slightly classical.
- Use accurate editable typography. Tibetan script should be verified and relatively restrained, not huge repeated ornament in every tile. Chinese can appear sparingly. Never invent Tibetan text; use a font that supports Tibetan glyphs.
- Relevant motifs may include Gesang flowers, yak contours, mountain ridges, roads, monastery/stupa silhouettes, and prayer-flag cords. Vary motifs and ground them in the photos/context.

## Each tile is a photomontage
Each tile may combine two or more distinct source photos:
1. A dominant foreground photo, cropped large and allowed to cover other content.
2. A secondary photo, detail crop, or ghosted underlay, partly obscured by the foreground.
3. An optional third fragment when it contributes to story or depth.
4. Sparse line art, verified typography, and limited black/red graphic marks.

Photos must visibly overlap and occlude each other. Do not simply place intact rectangular photos side-by-side on a blank sheet. Use irregular masks, edge-aware crops, cutouts where feasible, soft opacity changes, and controlled partial concealment. Some of an underlay may be hidden intentionally; keep enough visible to recognize its shape or subject.

## Layer hierarchy and tonal treatment
Assign each photo layer a role:
- Hero: clearest focal photo, near-natural color and strongest local contrast.
- Support: second scene/detail, slightly lower saturation or contrast if useful.
- Ghost: background photo used for shape, texture, ridge, sky, or atmosphere; muted and sometimes partially transparent.
- Bridge: photo or extracted silhouette deliberately spanning tile boundaries.

Do not apply a global grayscale filter. As loose starting points only, keep hero saturation around 0.80–1.00 of source, support 0.60–0.85, ghost 0.35–0.70; tune against the real images. The collage should remain colored, not muddy or monochrome. Opacity and saturation are separate controls.

## Background and edges
Use a clean neutral/cool near-white visually matched to pale parts of the source photos. Avoid a universal warm-paper color. Photography should occupy most of each tile; reduce outer margins and gutters.

Do not use rough brush edges as the default mask. If a brush edge is used, match its value and hue to the photo edge it touches (for example, pale grey-white against cloudy sky). Prefer irregular masks, soft transitions, partial fades, edge-aware blending, and overlap. Avoid conspicuous white brush outlines around dark photos and avoid repeating the same brush treatment nine times.

## Cross-tile continuity through shared layers
Plan the full 3×3 canvas in one coordinate system before slicing into nine tiles. Strong continuity can come from a shared image layer, not only a line:
- One source photograph cropped/scaled across two or three neighboring tiles, with its visible parts split naturally by tile boundaries.
- A long Tibetan text line, mountain contour, road, Gesang flower stem, prayer-flag cord, or red stroke placed once on the full canvas and sliced afterward.

For example, a single photo layer can span the right half of tile A and the left half of tile B. Its geometry is authored once on the full canvas so the split aligns. Do not independently resize the same image inside A and B. The bridge photo can connect distinct tile compositions; do not repeat one identical hero photo across a whole row by default.

## Tile diversity and center
Every tile should have its own photo combination, crop, layer order, and focal balance. Avoid repeating the same base landscape in neighboring cells unless it is intentionally a shared continuous layer. Do not automatically make the center tile a blank title panel: give it a substantive photo montage, with supporting fragments and restrained graphics. Keep Tibetan text sparse and varied in placement/scale. Use whitespace selectively rather than leaving large empty white areas.

## Render-plan needs
A future multi-layer plan should describe canvas-wide layers (source image, transform/crop, opacity, saturation, contrast, mask, z-order), tile-local layers (source image, crop, role, tonal treatment, mask, z-order), typography and line-art layers, background/palette, and export boundaries. Keep originals untouched and never commit private source photos to the repository.

## Review checklist
- Most tiles visibly combine multiple distinct photos where suitable.
- Overlap/occlusion reads as photomontage, not tidy photo cards.
- Colors remain muted but alive.
- Background is clean/cool near-white and blends with pale photo edges.
- Photo coverage is generous; gutters/margins are narrow.
- Brush edges do not clash with photo edge tones.
- Selected shared photo/text/line-art layers cross boundaries and align after slicing.
- Tile combinations and motifs vary; Tibetan text is verified and restrained.
- Center tile has visual substance.
